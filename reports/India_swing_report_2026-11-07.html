How the list was built

  1. Universe. Nifty 500 constituents (NSE archive file), with two years of daily prices from Yahoo Finance.
  2. Tradability filter. Average daily turnover above ₹25 crore, daily range between 1.2% and 6.5%, RSI below 80, and no more than 16% above the 20-day average.
  3. Technical score. 35% six-month return, 25% three-month return, 25% closeness to the 52-week high, 15% accumulation volume, with penalties for overbought, extended or below-50-day charts. Top 70 kept.
  4. Fundamental overlay. Analyst target upside (only with 5+ analysts), recommendation mean, revenue and earnings growth, operating margin, valuation, leverage, and the stock's move after its latest earnings date. Earnings inside the window are penalised.
  5. News and catalyst check of the finalists: headline scan plus a web search on each latest quarterly result.
  6. Final selection. No sector above about 41% of weight, a two-year risk review of each stock, and weights inverse to 25-day volatility, capped at 10% and floored at 3.5%.

Walk-forward test of the technical score

The same ranking was applied at 22 past dates over two years. The top 15 were held for the next 25 trading days.

Whole universe0.46%
Top 15 by score4.10%

Average edge 3.64% per 25 days. 54% of the top-15 stocks finished positive, and the top 15 beat the universe in 16 of 22 windows. Latest window: the most recent window lost 3.1% while the universe gained 0.4%. The edge is real on average and unreliable in any single month.

The central outcome above is lower than 4.1% because the test uses today's index members (survivorship bias), covers a two-year bull run, tests only the technical part and has just 22 overlapping windows. I count at most half the edge on top of normal drift. The Monte Carlo below puts the odds of a positive five weeks at about 50–58% under realistic assumptions.

Not verifiable here: Yahoo analyst targets lag fast prices (several sit at or below the current price); some Indian news items came from aggregator sites that disagree on figures, which the text flags; there is no live intraday or options feed.

Scenario and stress analysis

Monte Carlo

Monte Carlo: 20,000 five-week paths per row, built by resampling the basket's own daily returns in 5-day blocks (this keeps the correlation between stocks). Each stock's past drift was removed first, so the numbers show risk without the selection bias of picking recent winners. The plan rows apply the stops (daily close), sell 40% at Target 1 and move the stop to entry, and sell the rest at Target 2, with 0.1% slippage each side.
