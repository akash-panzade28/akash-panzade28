1. Policy shock. Top Democrats called for a national data-center moratorium after the midterms (CNBC, Nov 5), and 41 data-center ballot measures went before voters in 10 states, with 56% of voters in a CNBC poll calling data centers a crucial or major factor. Morgan Stanley said decisive Democratic results carry "greater expectations for data center restrictions". The brake is the presidential veto, and PBS notes a tougher regulatory climate is expected even if a federal crackdown stalls. OpenAI's CFO said on Nov 6 it will press ahead with US expansion; it also published community commitments on power costs and ended data-center NDAs.

2. Demand questions at the edges. Bloomberg reported on Nov 5–6 that Microsoft is tightening oversight of neocloud projects and winding down a 2023 deal to rent AI capacity from Oracle. Oracle says demand remains strong and some outlets frame it as a hardware-fit issue. The Wall Street Journal reported OpenAI leaders discussed resetting next year's capacity plans, but no decision was reported. These are single-source stories; they help explain why AI-cloud stocks are 55% to 78% below their highs.

3. Memory cycle. DRAM contract prices hit another record in October and TrendForce expects DRAM including HBM up 15% to 20% this quarter, but NAND contract prices stalled at $30.61 after a 21-month rise, Kioxia gave a conservative December-quarter forecast on Nov 2, and handset makers are struggling with component costs (secondary sources cite a 12.9% fall in 2026 smartphone shipments). Micron fell about 30% and Sandisk about 39% from Oct 29 to Nov 5.

4. Capex is still rising. Guidance for 2026: Amazon about $220B, Alphabet $195–205B, Microsoft roughly $170–175B (distorted by an accounting change) and Meta $135–145B, about 78% above 2025. Nvidia guided its Nov 17 quarter to about $108B, up from $96.2B.

5. Software is a separate story. The software ETF is down 10% in a month versus 27% for chips. The worry is AI agents eroding per-seat software: Atlassian fell 12% on slower growth and ServiceNow announced layoffs after a 35% slide. Chip-design and data-platform software are treated differently from application software in this list.

How the list was built

  1. Universe. 129 US-listed AI, semiconductor, memory, networking, infrastructure, hyperscaler, software and cybersecurity tickers (126 with usable data), with two years of daily prices from Yahoo Finance.
  2. Filters. Average daily turnover above $80 million, price above $5, and daily range above 1.2% (removes price-pinned deal stocks).
  3. Score. Revenue growth, operating margin, valuation versus growth, analyst target upside (with five or more analysts), recommendation mean, distance from the 52-week high, and a bounce test (RSI under 35 with a positive day, versus still falling). Penalties for extreme volatility and for earnings inside the window.
  4. Cross-checks. SEC filings for 18 of the 20 names through trader-analyst, headlines through trader-news, and a web search on each theme (policy, capex, memory pricing, software disruption, Nvidia).
  5. Selection. Judgement across the value chain so no layer dominates, weights inverse to 25-day volatility (capped at 9%, floored at 2.5%), and one or two event names at most.

Execution plan

  1. Size. Treat the whole basket as one position. It behaves like roughly 1.5x to 2x the S&P 500, and if every stop hit it would lose about 14%. At 5% of a portfolio, that is a worst planned loss of about 0.7% of the portfolio; at 10%, about 1.4%.
  2. Entry in thirds. First third from Monday in the buy zone. Second third on Nov 18 only if the S&P 500 is above 7,000 and the stock is above its stop. Third third by Nov 25 under the same test. Names that have not bounced (Western Digital) wait for a close above their Nov 6 close.
  3. Stops. Daily-close stops at the listed levels; never widen one.
  4. Profit-taking. Sell a third to a half at Target 1, move the stop on the rest to entry, then trail under the 20-day average toward Target 2.
  5. Kill-switch. If the S&P 500 closes the week below 7,000 or the VIX closes above 32 (its 52-week high is 31.0), cut the whole basket in half and stop adding.
  6. Time stop. At the end of week 3, exit any position below its average entry and below its 20-day average.
  7. Exit. Close everything by Mon Dec 7, before the Fed and Broadcom's Dec 9 report. Hold to Dec 11 only if all remaining positions are above their trailing stops.
  8. If you invest from India, US stocks need a broker with international access (LRS), returns also move with the rupee, and tax treatment differs; check with a professional.
