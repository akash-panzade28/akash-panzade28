## 3. How the list was built, and how much to trust it

1. **Universe:** Nifty 500 constituents (NSE archive file); two years of daily prices from Yahoo Finance for each.
2. **Tradability filter:** average daily turnover above ₹25 crore, daily range between 1.2% and 6.5% (removes deal-pinned or extremely wild names), RSI below 80, and not more than 16% above the 20-day average.
3. **Technical score** (35% 6-month return, 25% 3-month return, 25% closeness to the 52-week high, 15% accumulation volume, with penalties for overbought, extended or below-50-day-average charts). Top 70 kept.
4. **Fundamental overlay** on those 70 (Yahoo Finance quote-summary data): analyst mean target upside (only counted with 5+ analysts), recommendation mean, revenue and earnings growth, operating margin, valuation, leverage, and what the stock did after its latest earnings date. Names with earnings **inside** the window are penalised, because that is a coin-flip event.
5. **News and catalyst check** for the top ~28 names: headline scan, plus web searches on each finalist's latest quarterly results.
6. **Final selection** with sector caps (no sector above ~41% of the weight: see the sector mix in section 4) and a two-year risk review of each stock (volatility, beta, and how often it hit its own stop or targets). Weights are inverse to 25-day volatility, capped at 10% and floored at 3.5%.

**Walk-forward test of the technical score (what the evidence says):** I applied the same technical ranking at 22 past dates over the last two years, took the top 15, and measured the next 25 trading days. The top 15 returned **4.10%** on average versus **0.46%** for the whole universe: an average edge of **3.64%**, with 54% of top-15 stocks finishing positive and the top 15 beating the universe in 16 of 22 windows. Note the latest window: the most recent window lost 3.1% while the universe gained 0.4%. So the edge is real on average but not reliable in any single month.

**Why the expectation above is lower than +4.1%:** that test (a) uses today's index members, so it carries survivorship bias, (b) covers a two-year bull run, (c) tests only the technical part, and (d) has just 22 overlapping windows. I therefore count at most half of the edge, on top of normal market drift. Section 7 shows a 20,000-path Monte Carlo that puts the odds of a positive five weeks at about 50-58% under realistic assumptions.

**What I could not verify:** Yahoo Finance's analyst targets lag fast-moving prices (several mean targets sit at or below the current price), many Indian news items came from aggregator sites that sometimes disagree on figures (I flag these in the text), and I have no live feed for intraday prices or options data.

## 7. Scenario and stress analysis

**Monte Carlo.** Monte Carlo: 20,000 five-week paths per row, built by resampling the basket's own daily returns in 5-day blocks (this keeps the correlation between stocks). Each stock's past drift was removed first, so the numbers show risk without the selection bias of picking recent winners. The plan rows apply the stops (daily close), sell 40% at Target 1 and move the stop to entry, and sell the rest at Target 2, with 0.1% slippage each side.

| Assumed drift per 25 days | Method | Mean | Median | 5th pct | 95th pct | Chance positive | Chance of losing 5%+ | Worst of 20,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0% (pure risk, no edge) | Stops and targets | -0.4% | -0.6% | -6.6% | +7.0% | 44% | 13% | -10.2% |
| 0% (pure risk, no edge) | No stops (hold) | -0.3% | -0.5% | -8.9% | +8.9% | 46% | 19% | -18.3% |
| +0.8% (typical market drift per 25 days) | Stops and targets | +0.2% | -0.1% | -6.3% | +7.5% | 49% | 11% | -9.8% |
| +0.8% (typical market drift per 25 days) | No stops (hold) | +0.5% | +0.3% | -8.3% | +9.9% | 52% | 16% | -19.8% |
| +2% (market drift plus about half the past screen edge) | Stops and targets | +1.0% | +0.9% | -5.6% | +8.2% | 58% | 7% | -9.5% |
| +2% (market drift plus about half the past screen edge) | No stops (hold) | +1.6% | +1.5% | -7.3% | +11.1% | 61% | 11% | -20.2% |

Read it this way: with no edge at all the chance of finishing positive is about 44-46%. At a typical market drift it is about 49-52%. It reaches 58-61% only if the screen keeps about half of its past edge plus normal drift. The stops and targets lower the average by 0.1 to 0.6 points but cut the 5th-percentile loss from about −8% to about −6% and the worst case from −18% to −9%.
