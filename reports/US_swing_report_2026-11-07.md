## 2. Market backdrop and what it means

**Where the market is (Nov 6 close):** the S&P 500 is at 7,377, down 5.0% in a month and below its 50-day average (7,679) but still above its 200-day average (7,325). The Nasdaq Composite is at 24,579, down 9.6% in a month, and went into correction territory on Thursday Nov 5 (a drop of about 5.5%). The VIX closed at 25.6 after topping 28, the highest since March (CNBC, Schwab).

**What caused it:** fear about AI spending after Democrats won the midterms, in particular that a Democratic Congress could slow or regulate data-center buildout (CNBC, Nov 5; Schwab). Jefferies called the drop "reactionary, not a fundamental reset". A Bespoke study cited by CNBC found past VIX spikes of this size were often followed by positive 3- and 6-month S&P returns, but the average 1-month return afterwards was −0.4%, because a few cases kept falling.

**Rates are the second driver.** October payrolls came in at 165,000 (more than double forecasts), unemployment 4.1%, wage growth 3.1% (Reuters). Futures price about a 78% chance that the Fed **raises** rates by 25 bp on Dec 8–9 from 3.75–4.00% (Reuters via Business Recorder). Axios notes falling tech stocks and oil could eliminate a further hike.

**What this means for the pick list:** relative strength has been in defensives (healthcare, payments, staples), which are up 10–17% in the same month the index fell 5%. That rotation is the main crowding risk. So about 91% of the list is defensive/quality compounders and it keeps a deliberate ~9% tactical rebound sleeve in oversold AI-linked names (Arista, Zebra) that would benefit if the selloff reverses.

## 3. How the list was built, and how much to trust it

1. **Universe:** S&P 500 constituents (Wikipedia list); two years of daily prices from Yahoo Finance for each.
2. **Tradability filter:** average daily turnover above $50 million, daily range between 1.2% and 6.5% (removes deal-pinned or extremely wild names), RSI below 80, and not more than 16% above the 20-day average.
3. **Technical score** (35% 6-month return, 25% 3-month return, 25% closeness to the 52-week high, 15% accumulation volume, with penalties for overbought, extended or below-50-day-average charts). Top 70 kept.
4. **Fundamental overlay** on those 70 (Yahoo Finance quote-summary data): analyst mean target upside (only counted with 5+ analysts), recommendation mean, revenue and earnings growth, operating margin, valuation, leverage, and what the stock did after its latest earnings date. Names with earnings **inside** the window are penalised, because that is a coin-flip event.
5. **News and catalyst check** for the top ~28 names: headline scan, plus web searches on each finalist's latest quarterly results.
6. **Final selection** with sector caps (no sector above ~41% of the weight: see the sector mix in section 4) and a two-year risk review of each stock (volatility, beta, and how often it hit its own stop or targets). Weights are inverse to 25-day volatility, capped at 10% and floored at 3.5%.

**Walk-forward test of the technical score (what the evidence says):** I applied the same technical ranking at 22 past dates over the last two years, took the top 15, and measured the next 25 trading days. The top 15 returned **5.60%** on average versus **1.83%** for the whole universe: an average edge of **3.76%**, with 58% of top-15 stocks finishing positive and the top 15 beating the universe in 14 of 22 windows. Note the latest window: the most recent window lost 16.0% while the universe gained 3.5%. So the edge is real on average but not reliable in any single month.

**Why the expectation above is lower than +5.6%:** that test (a) uses today's index members, so it carries survivorship bias, (b) covers a two-year bull run, (c) tests only the technical part, and (d) has just 22 overlapping windows. I therefore count roughly half of the edge, on top of normal market drift.

**What I could not verify:** Yahoo Finance's analyst targets lag fast-moving prices (several mean targets sit at or below the current price), many Indian news items came from aggregator sites that sometimes disagree on figures (I flag these in the text), and I have no live feed for intraday prices or options data.

## 7. Execution plan

1. **Entry:** place limit orders inside each buy zone. For names marked "starter ⅓", buy only a third on Monday and wait for a pullback toward the 20-day average for the rest; if no pullback appears by Nov 20, add half of the remainder and skip the rest.
2. **Stops:** use daily-close stops at the listed levels. Do not widen a stop after entry.
3. **Taking profit:** sell a third to a half at Target 1, move the stop on the remainder to your entry price, then trail it under the 20-day average toward Target 2.
4. **Time stop:** at the end of week 3, exit any position that is below its entry price **and** below its 20-day average; the thesis is not working.
5. **Portfolio kill-switch:** if S&P 500 closes the week below 7,000 (about 5% below Friday's close), or the VIX closes above 32 (above its 52-week high of 31.0), cut every position by half and reassess.
6. **Weekly review (every Friday):** check news and any rating changes; replace a stock only if a stop is hit or its thesis breaks (for example a guidance cut), never because it is up.
7. **Exit:** close the remainder in the Dec 7–14 window. If you invest from India, US stocks need a broker that supports international investing (LRS), and your return will also move with the rupee/dollar rate; check taxes with a professional.
