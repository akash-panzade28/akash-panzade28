Where the market is (Nov 6 close): the S&P 500 is at 7,377, down 5.0% in a month and below its 50-day average (7,679) but still above its 200-day average (7,325). The Nasdaq Composite is at 24,579, down 9.6% in a month, and went into correction territory on Thursday Nov 5 (a drop of about 5.5%). The VIX closed at 25.6 after topping 28, the highest since March (CNBC, Schwab).

What caused it: fear about AI spending after Democrats won the midterms, in particular that a Democratic Congress could slow or regulate data-center buildout (CNBC, Nov 5; Schwab). Jefferies called the drop "reactionary, not a fundamental reset". A Bespoke study cited by CNBC found past VIX spikes of this size were often followed by positive 3- and 6-month S&P returns, but the average 1-month return afterwards was −0.4%, because a few cases kept falling.

Rates are the second driver. October payrolls came in at 165,000 (more than double forecasts), unemployment 4.1%, wage growth 3.1% (Reuters). Futures price about a 78% chance that the Fed raises rates by 25 bp on Dec 8–9 from 3.75–4.00% (Reuters via Business Recorder). Axios notes falling tech stocks and oil could eliminate a further hike.

What this means for the pick list: relative strength has been in defensives (healthcare, payments, staples), which are up 10–17% in the same month the index fell 5%. That rotation is the main crowding risk. So about 91% of the list is defensive/quality compounders and it keeps a deliberate ~9% tactical rebound sleeve in oversold AI-linked names (Arista, Zebra) that would benefit if the selloff reverses.

How the list was built

  1. Universe. S&P 500 constituents (Wikipedia list), with two years of daily prices from Yahoo Finance.
  2. Tradability filter. Average daily turnover above $50 million, daily range between 1.2% and 6.5%, RSI below 80, and no more than 16% above the 20-day average.
  3. Technical score. 35% six-month return, 25% three-month return, 25% closeness to the 52-week high, 15% accumulation volume, with penalties for overbought, extended or below-50-day charts. Top 70 kept.
  4. Fundamental overlay. Analyst target upside (only with 5+ analysts), recommendation mean, revenue and earnings growth, operating margin, valuation, leverage, and the stock's move after its latest earnings date. Earnings inside the window are penalised.
  5. News and catalyst check of the finalists: headline scan plus a web search on each latest quarterly result.
  6. Final selection. No sector above about 41% of weight, a two-year risk review of each stock, and weights inverse to 25-day volatility, capped at 10% and floored at 3.5%.

Walk-forward test of the technical score

The same ranking was applied at 22 past dates over two years. The top 15 were held for the next 25 trading days.

Average edge 3.76% per 25 days. 58% of the top-15 stocks finished positive, and the top 15 beat the universe in 14 of 22 windows. Latest window: the most recent window lost 16.0% while the universe gained 3.5%. The edge is real on average and unreliable in any single month.

The central outcome above is lower than 5.6% because the test uses today's index members (survivorship bias), covers a two-year bull run, tests only the technical part and has just 22 overlapping windows. I count at most half the edge on top of normal drift. The Monte Carlo below puts the odds of a positive five weeks at about 55% under realistic assumptions.

Not verifiable here: Yahoo analyst targets lag fast prices (several sit at or below the current price); some Indian news items came from aggregator sites that disagree on figures, which the text flags; there is no live intraday or options feed.

Scenario and stress analysis

Monte Carlo

Monte Carlo: 20,000 five-week paths per row, built by resampling the basket's own daily returns in 5-day blocks (this keeps the correlation between stocks). Each stock's past drift was removed first, so the numbers show risk without the selection bias of picking recent winners. Execution plan

  1. Entry. Place limit orders inside each buy zone. For "starter third" names, buy a third on Monday and wait for a pullback toward the 20-day average; if none appears by Nov 20, add half of the remainder and skip the rest.
  2. Stops. Use daily-close stops at the listed levels and never widen one after entry.
  3. Taking profit. Sell a third to a half at Target 1, move the stop on the rest to your entry price, then trail under the 20-day average toward Target 2.
  4. Time stop. At the end of week 3, exit any position that is below its entry price and below its 20-day average.
  5. Kill-switch. If the S&P 500 closes the week below 7,000 (about 5% below Friday's close), or the VIX closes above 32 (above its 52-week high of 31.0), cut every position by half and reassess.
  6. Weekly review. Every Friday check news and rating changes. Replace a stock only if a stop is hit or its thesis breaks, never because it is up.
  7. Exit. Close the remainder by Mon Dec 7, the last session before the Fed (US) and the Dec 4 RBI decision (India). Hold to Dec 11 only if every remaining position is above its trailing stop.
